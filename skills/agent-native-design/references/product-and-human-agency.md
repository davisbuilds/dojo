# Product and Human Agency

Use when agents are intended consumers or intermediaries and the product boundary
is still being chosen. Keep technical access, useful delegation, and a viable
offering as separate questions; success at one does not establish the others.

## Identify the responsibility and its beneficiaries

Clarify which job the product takes responsibility for, what an agent consumes,
and who benefits from its successful use. The operator, beneficiary, purchaser,
and agent developer can be different people. A personal tool may have no separate
payer; do not turn every design into a commercialization exercise.

For example, a scheduling service might serve requests from customers' assistants,
help a business fill appointments, and charge the business. The assistant needs
availability, constraints, reservation semantics, and dependable status. The
business needs correct bookings and manageable exceptions. A conversational UI
alone would not answer either set of needs.

Consider a bounded capability, managed outcome, or end-user product according to
the requested scope. Do not assume that enabling agents requires a new platform,
a universal assistant, a multi-agent system, or a monorepo. Existing software may
supply most of the capability; ownership of the unresolved responsibility is the
useful design question.

## Give people meaningful control

Human agency includes setting priorities, defining discretion, understanding
consequences, changing course, and recovering. Approval prompts are one control,
not its definition. A person should be able to delegate routine choices without
being asked to reconstruct every step, and to inspect a consequential decision
without reading an unfiltered transcript.

Choose the interaction the decision needs: an exception with evidence, a preview
of a consequential change, a concise outcome receipt, or a place to revise an
ongoing responsibility. Show material uncertainty and what has already taken
effect. Match freshness and notification behavior to how the person uses that
information; a separate real-time dashboard is not automatically useful.

If agent and human interfaces offer the same outcome, keep their underlying
state and business rules consistent. Their interactions can differ. An agent may
need structured status and correlation IDs where a person needs a clear summary
and a way to intervene. A human-only action can remain human-only by design.

## Design for stronger agents without assuming the market

Ask what improves as models become more capable and what the surrounding product
still supplies: authoritative context, access to real systems, reliable effects,
coordination, evidence, or a trusted customer relationship. Those are possible
sources of value, not guaranteed defensibility.

Consider both a dominant assistant that delegates and a landscape of independent
agents. A cross-provider service needs a concrete coordination problem; supporting
multiple model APIs alone does not demonstrate one. Check supported integration
access and current alternatives before relying on a platform gap.

Separate temporary scaffolding from durable contracts. Keep prompts, model
choices, and replaceable integrations easy to revise where feasible. Compare
changes on representative work before assuming that a model upgrade permits the
removal of recovery or authority controls. Avoid freezing every current model
weakness into the product's permanent structure.

## Look for evidence of the promised value

Evaluate a complete responsibility from the beneficiary's perspective. Was the
result accepted? How much attention, correction, delay, and expense did it require?
Did exception handling make the work easier to steer? A demo, tool-call count,
or autonomous completion rate alone cannot answer those questions.

For an exploratory idea, distinguish feasibility evidence from demand evidence.
Real workflow observations, adoption, and willingness to pay can change the
product decision. Broad forecasts and other companies' reported productivity
are hypotheses to investigate, not proof of this offering's value. Use current
primary sources for external claims consequential to the decision.
